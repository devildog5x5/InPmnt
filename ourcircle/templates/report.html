If you already sent money or shared information

You are not alone, and speed helps. Work down this list. Do not send more money to “fix” the first payment.

  1. Call the bank or card issuer using the number on the back of the card or a paper statement — not a number from the scam. Ask to freeze the card and dispute the charge.
  2. If you wired money or used Zelle / crypto / gift cards, tell that provider immediately that it was fraud. Gift cards and crypto are often unrecoverable; still report them.
  3. Change passwords on email and the account that was named, from a device the caller never touched. Turn on two-factor authentication.
  4. Report to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov.
  5. Internet crime (wires, crypto, phishing): IC3.gov.
  6. Social Security or IRS impersonation: hang up, then use the numbers published on ssa.gov or irs.gov — never a callback from the message.
  7. Tell your circle so they do not fall for a follow-up “we can get your money back” pitch.
  8. Consider a credit freeze at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion if a Social Security number or ID was shared.
